# Contributing to Argyll

Argyll is a technically governed, politically neutral open-source project. Its purpose is to build excellent software according to a coherent technical vision.

Contributors are welcome regardless of background, identity, nationality, political belief, or personal affiliation. What matters here is the quality of the work, the quality of the collaboration, and alignment with Argyll's goals.

These guidelines apply to Argyll's repositories, issue trackers, discussions, chat spaces, documentation, events, and other official project infrastructure.

## Technical Merit

Contributions are evaluated on their technical merit and value to Argyll. Relevant considerations include:

- Correctness and reliability
- Clarity and maintainability
- Security and operational safety
- Performance and resource efficiency
- Compatibility with the architecture
- Usefulness to users
- Quality of tests and documentation
- Long-term maintenance cost
- Alignment with the project's scope and direction

Reputation, seniority, popularity, and volume of participation do not entitle anyone to have a proposal accepted. A contributor's personal beliefs, identity, and affiliations are irrelevant to evaluating their work.

A technically sound contribution may still be declined when it conflicts with Argyll's direction, introduces disproportionate complexity, creates an unacceptable maintenance burden, or compromises the coherence of the system. A good patch is not automatically the right patch for Argyll.

## Political Neutrality

Contributors are free to hold and express their own political, ideological, religious, and cultural views in their personal lives. Those views should be left outside Argyll.

Official project spaces and infrastructure must not be used for unrelated political advocacy, campaigning, ideological declarations, or cultural disputes. Argyll will not adopt institutional positions on matters unrelated to its software, users, contributors, or ability to operate.

No contributor will be required to endorse a political position, make an ideological declaration, or pass a viewpoint test as a condition of participation. Decisions about contributions and participation will be based on work performed within Argyll and conduct that materially affects it.

Technical decisions can have social, legal, security, and ethical consequences. Discussing those consequences is appropriate when they are directly relevant to the software. Such discussions should remain concrete, evidence-based, and connected to an actual project decision.

## Professional Conduct

Healthy technical disagreement is welcome. Contributors may challenge assumptions, reject proposals, identify mistakes, and argue strongly for a better approach. Criticism should address the work and the reasoning behind it.

Contributors must not:

- Harass, threaten, intimidate, or personally attack others
- Deliberately disrupt project work or communication
- Misrepresent another person's statements or contributions
- Publish private information without permission
- Repeatedly derail technical discussions with unrelated disputes
- Abuse project access, infrastructure, credentials, or authority
- Discriminate in project decisions on grounds unrelated to merit or conduct

Courtesy is expected, but agreement is not. No contributor has a right to be insulated from technical criticism, rejection, or a final decision with which they disagree.

## Decision-Making and the BDFL

Argyll is led by its Benevolent Dictator for Life (BDFL). The BDFL is the final authority on:

- Project vision and scope
- Architecture and technical direction
- Acceptance or rejection of contributions
- Maintainer roles and repository access
- Releases and compatibility policy
- Interpretation and enforcement of these guidelines

Open discussion and reasoned dissent are encouraged. Contributors earn influence through sound judgment, useful work, and sustained stewardship. Consensus is desirable when it produces the right result, but it is not required. When consensus cannot be reached, the BDFL makes the final decision.

Argyll is meritocratic, but it is not a democracy. Contribution creates influence, not ownership of the project's direction or an entitlement to decision-making authority.

The BDFL may delegate authority to maintainers. Delegated authority may be changed or withdrawn when necessary to protect the technical quality, security, continuity, or direction of Argyll.

## Maintainers

Maintainers are expected to apply these guidelines consistently and in good faith. They should explain consequential technical decisions when practical, distinguish personal preference from project requirements, and use their access only for the responsibilities entrusted to them.

Maintainer status reflects current responsibility, judgment, and contribution. It is not an honorary title or permanent entitlement.

Conflicts of interest that could materially affect a project decision should be disclosed. When appropriate, the affected maintainer should allow another maintainer or the BDFL to make the decision.

## Enforcement

Problems should be raised privately with a maintainer or directly with the BDFL whenever public discussion would worsen the situation or expose sensitive information.

Responses will be proportionate to the behavior and its effect on Argyll. They may include clarification, a warning, moderation of a discussion, rejection or reversal of a contribution, temporary restrictions, removal of project access, or exclusion from project spaces.

Enforcement concerns conduct within Argyll and conduct outside it only when that conduct directly and materially threatens contributors, users, project security, legal operation, or the integrity of project resources. Disagreement with a person's lawful beliefs or associations is not, by itself, grounds for action.

The BDFL has final authority over enforcement decisions.

## The Standard

Bring good work. Make sound arguments. Treat collaborators as capable adults. Keep unrelated politics outside Argyll. Accept that not every contribution will be adopted and that final responsibility requires final authority.

The goal is not ideological conformity or frictionless agreement. The goal is excellent software.
